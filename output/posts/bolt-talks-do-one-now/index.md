I had the privilage to participate in a <a href="http://cfmumbojumbo.com/cf/index.cfm/bolttalks/">Bolt Talk</a> with <a href="http://cfmumbojumbo.com/cf/index.cfm/who-am-i/">Tim Cunningham</a>. If you are passionate about a project, topic, or pretty much anything related to programming, do one now. All you need to do is <a href="http://cfmumbojumbo.com/cf/index.cfm/who-am-i/">contact Tim</a>. It was a great experience and easy since Tim handles recording and editing.
<h3>BoltTalk</h3>
"A BoltTalk is a short podcast with a single interview on a single narrowly focused topic of interest to people in the programming community. If you would like to talk to me for a few minutes on any topic that would be of interest to the programming community, for less than 15 minutes, please submit them to me via gmail: timcunningham71 . This article gives you more background on the genesis and purpose of a BoltTalk."
<h3>Cloudy with a chance of tests<br /></h3>
Mike Henke talks about trying to introduce continuous integration and unit testing into his legacy code. Taking a "baby steps" approach he tells how he sets up a continuous integration environment, to test code changes even without unit testing. <a href="http://cfmumbojumbo.com/cf/index.cfm/bolttalks/bolt-talk-mike-henke-cloudy-with-a-chance-of-tests/">Bolt Talk: Mike Henke - Cloudy with a chance of tests</a>

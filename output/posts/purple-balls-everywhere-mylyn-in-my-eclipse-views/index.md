<img src="/images/pb1.jpg" alt="" width="600.0" height="609.0" />

<span>Those purple balls are <a href="http://www.eclipse.org/mylyn/">Mylyn</a> focus buttons. You may not have even noticed them in Eclipse before. Think of them as the <a href="http://media.staples.com/easybutton/demo.html">Staple's Easy button</a>. The purple balls will focus the view on </span>resources relevant to the task you are working on.

<span>Another example of "focus" is this: Think of my work desk. It is cluttered with papers, books, and other office resources. If my "desk view" had a Mylyn focus button, all the clutter would disappear and only the resources I need at a particular time would be available. The hidden resources didn't get put into the office trash can. The resources are just not seen, reducing the clutter.</span>

<span>What does "focus" mean to you as a programmer? Well, a picture is worth a thousand words. </span><strong>[Insert Another Brett Farve &amp; Vikings joke here]</strong>

Notice, with Mylyn Focus toggled on in the Navigator view, I only see important files for the task at hand.
<span>You may wonder how do the purple balls know what is relevant? This is done by Mylyn's Task Focused Interface. You create and activate a task, then start working with resources in Eclipse. The resources (or context) get remembered by the task as you work.</span>

<span>This context will remain with the task and should work with any view containing the purple balls. Check out </span><span><a href="http://cfeclipse.org/index.cfm/blog/cfeclipse-feature-review-tasks/">CFEclipse Feature Review: Tasks</a> </span><span>for more information on the Task view (not to be confused with the Task List view). When I tap the purple balls in the Task view and activate them, only relevant tasks will show.</span>

<span>Here you can see all the purple balls circled if you missed one from the original picture. They are in these views: Breakpoints, Navigator, Task List, Tasks, Problems, Outline, and Debug.</span>

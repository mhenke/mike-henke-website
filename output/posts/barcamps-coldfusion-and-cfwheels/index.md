I attended the <a href="http://www.barcampomaha.com">Omaha BarCamp</a> 2010 this past Sat. The <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BarCamp">BarCamps</a> I have attended are a great way to meet passionate creative people in your location. BarCamps are an unconference with user generated content. Whomever shows up can present on a topic they are passionate about. Here is a more about the <a href="http://www.siliconprairienews.com/2010/11/barcamp-omaha-2010-schedule-plus-morning-photos">BarCamp Omaha 2010 Schedule (plus morning photos)</a>.
 
I presented a ColdFusion topic, ColdFusion On Wheels (Stealing Ideas from Rails). I had about a dozen people in my session. I did a quick dis-spelling of ColdFusion misconceptions like it is a relic, costs money, etc then talked a little about <a href="http://www.cfwheels.org">CFWheels</a>. The session was only 30 mins so I kept it light.
 
 I had a great positive time at the camp, except the Omaha BarCamp is organized by a couple Rails guys.  As the announcer read my topic, he ad-libbed some snide comment about ColdFusion being outdated. I was prepared so at the start of my session I had wikipedia pulled up with <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ColdFusion">ColdFusion</a> and <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_%28programming_language%29">Ruby</a> summaries. Basically, both languages appeared in 1995 and have had recent releases within the last 6 months. As for Rails verses any ColdFusion framework, I assume the same can be said.
 
I also mentioned ColdFusion has different engines so you have choices between open source like <a href="http://www.getrailo.com">Railo</a> or <a href="http://www.openbluedragon.org">Open Blue Dragon</a> or proprietary like <a href="http://www.adobe.com/products/coldfusion">Adobe ColdFusion</a>. Finally I mentioned if you don't like the tag syntax, you have the option for more of a scripting syntax. 
 
<h2>Attend One in your location!!!</h2>
I would recommend anyone checking the web for a BarCamp near you, attend, and present something over ColdFusion.
